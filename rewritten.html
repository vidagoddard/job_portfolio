Roles & Contributions

From UX Design to Collaborative Leadership

UX Design · Information Architecture · Project Coordination

Rewritten was developed as a group project for the Digital Product Design for Cultural & Creative Sectors module at King's College London.

My contributions included:

  • Information Architecture: Structured the app's content and navigation to make East London's heritage collections easier to discover and explore.
  • User Flow: Mapped the journey from entering the app to exploring archival content and contributing community stories.
  • UI/UX Design: Designed sections of the Figma prototype and contributed extensively to the app's overall concept, features and user experience.
  • Visual Identity & Cultural Design: Took primary responsibility for developing the app's visual direction, adapting the British Library's existing design language into a more approachable, community-focused experience. Incorporated culturally relevant imagery, local landmarks and visual references to reflect East London's diverse communities.
  • Project Coordination & Leadership: Took the initiative to reorganise the team's workflow when the project fell behind schedule. Identified individual strengths, interests and development goals to distribute responsibilities effectively, support collaboration and keep the project moving towards completion.

This project allowed me to combine user-centred design, visual storytelling and collaborative leadership, while developing an experience that connects cultural heritage with the communities it represents.

Overview

Making large heritage collections accessible

The British Library holds one of the world's most extraordinary collections — yet for many local London communities, it can feel distant, institutional, and hard to navigate. This project tackled that gap directly.

Set within the module Digital Product Development for the Cultural Heritage & Creative Sector, the brief challenged us to reimagine how East London residents could discover and engage with neighbourhood history collections held by the Library — communities whose stories are in the archive, but who rarely see themselves reflected in how it's presented.

Design response

The design response centred on three principles: mobile-first accessibility, multilingual support, and community contribution — putting residents not just as users, but as active participants in how local history is told.

Problem

Understanding the Gap: Community Perception & Digital Barriers

The British Library holds rich local history, but for many East London residents, it remains out of reach.

What our research found (done by our group member):

  • The website uses technical language, complex filters, and catalogue codes that assume prior academic knowledge
  • Multilingual support is absent, excluding a significant portion of East London's diverse communities
  • The physical library is widely perceived as tourist-facing and intimidating — words like "daunting" and "not for locals" appeared repeatedly in user reviews
  • Many residents didn't know certain neighborhoods and everyday heritage content existed at all

The opportunity:
Despite this, research showed the Library's digital platforms already see strong engagement, meaning users are willing to interact online, even if the physical space feels inaccessible. We designed for that opening.

Design · Information architecture · Process

Information architecture

Rewritten is built around a flat, task-oriented structure, designed to reduce cognitive load and make archive content discoverable without prior knowledge.

Five primary navigation sections:

  • Home — featured stories and direct access to Explore East London, a borough-by-borough gateway to archives, stories, and quizzes
  • Search — tag-based filtering by borough, community, media type, and time period; tags visualised as topic bubbles to surface thematic connections
  • Map — an interactive alternative to traditional browsing, letting users navigate archive content geographically by borough
  • Events — a calendar view plus a dedicated Kids Corner, extending engagement beyond the app
  • Profile — personalisation through saved favourites, encouraging return visits and deeper involvement

Key structural decisions:

  • Flat hierarchy mirrors the British Library's existing website layout, giving users a sense of familiarity while reducing friction
  • Shallow navigation keeps intervening layers minimal, supporting task completion and discoverability
  • Multilingual support and text-to-speech available across all sections, reflecting East London's diverse communities

User flow

The core user journey is intentionally simple, designed to get residents into the archive with as little friction as possible.

Entry
Users choose to log in or continue as a guest — removing the barrier of mandatory account creation to avoid losing casual users before they've even started.

Home
From the homepage, three paths are available:

  • Explore the Archive — the primary journey
  • Browse the community database
  • Adjust display settings (light/dark mode)

Exploring the Archive
Selecting Explore redirects users to the Map tab, where they can navigate East London either through the interactive map or a borough list — two entry points for different user preferences. From here users access the full archive and story database.

Community Contribution
At any point in the archive experience, users can share their own story — the defining feature of Rewritten. After submitting, the feed refreshes and the loop begins again, encouraging continued engagement.

Aesthetic Design

Exploring interface directions

Rewritten is designed as an extension of the British Library's existing digital presence — not a departure from it. Visual continuity was a deliberate choice to build user confidence and reduce the learning curve for residents already familiar with the Library's website.

Visual Language
The British Library's interface uses soft rounded containers, neutral backgrounds, and pops of primary colour — a design system we carried directly into Rewritten. Soft curves are retained intentionally, as research indicates they feel less intimidating to users — aligned with the app's core goal of approachability.

Typography
The Library's native typeface (Segoe UI Semilight) was adapted for Figma using Inter, the closest available equivalent, maintaining the same clean, accessible reading experience across platforms.

Representing East London
Where Rewritten diverges from the Library's website is in its imagery. Archive image banners are reinterpreted to reflect East London specifically — borough outlines, local landmarks, and cultural references replace generic content. Examples include Whitechapel Market photography and Bengali tiger cloth patterns, grounding the app visually in the communities it serves rather than presenting a generic institutional aesthetic.

This approach transforms the design from simply accessible to actively welcoming. Telling East London residents visually that this space was made for them.

Reflection & Next Steps

Leading with Empathy, Designing with Intention

Rewritten taught me the importance of taking initiative, trusting my judgement and recognising the value of my own contributions within a collaborative environment.

Stepping into a project coordination role was particularly formative. I discovered that leadership is something I both enjoy and approach naturally, with an emphasis on understanding people rather than simply delegating tasks. I sought to identify each team member's strengths, interests and development goals, then align responsibilities with both individual preferences and the project's needs. This experience reinforced my belief that effective collaboration comes from creating an environment where people feel supported, valued and able to contribute meaningfully.

When the project encountered challenges, I also learned the importance of remaining composed, reassuring the team and maintaining a shared focus on our objectives. Balancing empathy with practical decision-making helped me support the group and keep the project moving forward.

Given more time, I would revisit the interface to refine certain visual elements and simplify features that may introduce unnecessary complexity. I would also prioritise usability testing to evaluate whether the navigation and information architecture effectively support the needs of different users.

Ultimately, Rewritten strengthened my confidence in taking responsibility, contributing my perspective and bringing people together to deliver a shared vision.
